Chemistry Calculator Guide

Understanding Common Chemistry Calculations

Molarity Calculations

Molarity (M) is a measure of the concentration of a solute in a solution. It's calculated as the number of moles of solute per liter of solution. The formula is M = moles / liters. This is one of the most important calculations in chemistry, especially when preparing solutions in the laboratory.

pH and pOH

pH is a logarithmic scale used to specify the acidity or basicity of an aqueous solution. pH = -log[H+], where [H+] is the concentration of hydrogen ions. The pH scale runs from 0 to 14, with 7 being neutral. Values below 7 indicate acidity, while values above 7 indicate basicity.

Molecular Weight

Molecular weight (or molar mass) is the sum of the atomic weights of all atoms in a molecule. It's expressed in atomic mass units (amu) or grams per mole (g/mol). Knowing the molecular weight is essential for converting between moles and grams and for many other chemistry calculations.

Stoichiometry

Stoichiometry deals with the quantitative relationships between the reactants and products in a chemical reaction. It uses balanced chemical equations to determine how much product will be formed from given amounts of reactants, or to identify the limiting reactant.

Limiting Reactants

In any chemical reaction, one reactant will be completely consumed before the others. This is called the limiting reactant because it limits the amount of product that can be formed. Identifying the limiting reactant and calculating the theoretical yield is a fundamental skill in chemistry.

Solution Dilution

Dilution is the process of adding solvent to a solution to decrease its concentration. The dilution formula M₁V₁ = M₂V₂ relates the initial molarity and volume to the final molarity and volume. This is widely used in laboratory work to prepare solutions of desired concentrations.

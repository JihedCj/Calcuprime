Understanding Your Results

What is BMR?

Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) is the number of calories your body burns at complete rest to maintain vital functions like breathing, circulation, and cell production. This represents the minimum calories you need to survive.

What is TDEE?

Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE) is your BMR multiplied by your activity level. This represents the total calories you burn in a typical day, including exercise and daily activities. To maintain your current weight, eat at your TDEE.

Macronutrients Explained

  • Protein (4 calories/gram): Essential for muscle repair, growth, and maintenance. We recommend 30% of calories for most goals.
  • Carbohydrates (4 calories/gram): Primary energy source for workouts and brain function. We recommend 40% of calories.
  • Fats (9 calories/gram): Important for hormone production and nutrient absorption. We recommend 30% of calories.

BMR Calculation Methods

This calculator uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, which is considered one of the most accurate BMR formulas:

Men: BMR = (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) - (5 × age) + 5
Women: BMR = (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) - (5 × age) - 161

Activity Level Guide

  • Sedentary (1.2): Desk job, little to no exercise
  • Lightly Active (1.375): Light exercise 1-3 days/week, some walking
  • Moderately Active (1.55): Moderate exercise 3-5 days/week
  • Very Active (1.725): Hard exercise 6-7 days/week
  • Extra Active (1.9): Very hard exercise, physical job, training twice per day

Tips for Achieving Your Calorie Goals

For Weight Loss:

  • Create a moderate calorie deficit (500-1000 calories/day)
  • Prioritize protein to preserve muscle mass
  • Track your food intake accurately
  • Be patient - sustainable weight loss is 0.5-1 kg per week
  • Don't go below 1200 calories (women) or 1500 calories (men)

For Muscle Gain:

  • Create a slight calorie surplus (250-500 calories/day)
  • Consume adequate protein (1.6-2.2g per kg body weight)
  • Focus on progressive overload in training
  • Be patient - lean muscle gain is slow (0.25-0.5 kg per week)

For Maintenance:

  • Eat at your TDEE
  • Monitor your weight weekly
  • Adjust calories if weight trends up or down
  • Focus on nutrient-dense whole foods

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Overestimating activity level: Most people should start with "lightly active" or "moderately active"
  • Not tracking accurately: Measure portions and log everything you eat
  • Cutting calories too low: This can slow metabolism and cause muscle loss
  • Ignoring protein: Adequate protein is crucial for body composition
  • Being impatient: Sustainable changes take time
  • Forgetting liquid calories: Track drinks, cooking oils, and condiments

Frequently Asked Questions

How accurate is this calorie calculator?

This calculator provides a scientifically-based estimate using the Mifflin-St Jeor equation. However, individual metabolic rates vary. Use these numbers as a starting point and adjust based on your results over 2-4 weeks.

Should I eat back exercise calories?

Your activity level already accounts for exercise. Only add extra calories if you do significantly more activity than usual or are an athlete with very high training volume.

How often should I recalculate my calories?

Recalculate every 5-10 kg of weight change, or if your activity level changes significantly.

Can I eat whatever I want as long as I hit my calorie target?

While calories matter most for weight change, food quality affects health, satiety, energy, and body composition. Focus on whole, nutrient-dense foods.

What if I'm not losing weight at my calculated deficit?

Give it 2-3 weeks, as weight fluctuates daily. If still no progress, reduce calories by 100-200 or increase activity slightly. Ensure you're tracking accurately.

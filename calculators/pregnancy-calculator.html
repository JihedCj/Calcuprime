Understanding Pregnancy Dating

Last Menstrual Period (LMP) Method

The LMP method is the most common way to calculate your due date. Healthcare providers typically use the first day of your last menstrual period as the starting point, even though conception usually occurs about 2 weeks later. This is why pregnancy is counted as 40 weeks (280 days) from the LMP.

Conception Date Method

If you know your conception date (from fertility tracking, IVF, or other methods), you can calculate your due date more precisely. Pregnancy from conception is approximately 38 weeks (266 days).

Naegele's Rule

The standard calculation follows Naegele's Rule:

Due Date = LMP + 280 days (40 weeks)
Or: LMP + 1 year - 3 months + 7 days

Pregnancy Trimesters

Pregnancy is divided into three trimesters, each approximately 13 weeks long:

First Trimester (Weeks 1-13)

  • Baby's development: Major organs and structures form. Heart begins beating around week 6. Fingers, toes, and facial features develop.
  • Your body: Hormonal changes cause fatigue, morning sickness, breast tenderness, and frequent urination.
  • Size: By week 12, baby is about 2.5 inches long (size of a lime).
  • Important milestones: First prenatal visit, dating ultrasound, possible genetic testing.

Second Trimester (Weeks 14-27)

  • Baby's development: Rapid growth. Baby can hear sounds, practice breathing, and move actively. Sex may be visible on ultrasound.
  • Your body: Energy returns, morning sickness usually subsides. Baby bump becomes visible. You may feel first movements (quickening) around weeks 18-20.
  • Size: By week 27, baby is about 14 inches long (size of a head of cauliflower).
  • Important milestones: Anatomy scan (18-22 weeks), glucose screening, feeling baby move.

Third Trimester (Weeks 28-40+)

  • Baby's development: Final growth and maturation. Lungs develop. Baby gains weight and gets into position for birth.
  • Your body: Increased discomfort, frequent urination returns, shortness of breath, Braxton Hicks contractions, preparing for labor.
  • Size: At term, baby is typically 19-21 inches long and weighs 6-9 pounds.
  • Important milestones: Regular prenatal visits increase, Group B strep test, birth plan discussions, hospital tour.

Common Pregnancy Symptoms by Trimester

First Trimester Symptoms

  • Morning sickness (nausea and vomiting)
  • Extreme fatigue and tiredness
  • Breast tenderness and swelling
  • Frequent urination
  • Food aversions or cravings
  • Mood swings
  • Light spotting or cramping
  • Constipation
  • Heightened sense of smell

Second Trimester Symptoms

  • Growing belly and weight gain
  • Feeling baby's movements
  • Back pain and round ligament pain
  • Skin changes (darkening, stretch marks)
  • Nasal congestion
  • Bleeding gums
  • Leg cramps
  • Braxton Hicks contractions (later in trimester)
  • Increased energy compared to first trimester

Third Trimester Symptoms

  • Shortness of breath
  • Frequent urination (baby pressing on bladder)
  • Heartburn and indigestion
  • Swelling in feet and ankles
  • Difficulty sleeping
  • More frequent Braxton Hicks contractions
  • Leaking colostrum from breasts
  • Baby "dropping" (lightening) before labor
  • Lower back pain and pelvic pressure

Essential Prenatal Care

Prenatal Visit Schedule

  • Weeks 4-28: One visit every 4 weeks
  • Weeks 28-36: One visit every 2 weeks
  • Weeks 36-40+: One visit every week until delivery

Important Prenatal Tests

  • Dating ultrasound (8-13 weeks): Confirms due date and checks for multiples
  • First trimester screening (11-14 weeks): Blood test and ultrasound for genetic conditions
  • Cell-free DNA screening (10+ weeks): Optional blood test for chromosomal conditions
  • Anatomy scan (18-22 weeks): Detailed ultrasound checking baby's development
  • Glucose screening (24-28 weeks): Tests for gestational diabetes
  • Group B strep test (35-37 weeks): Vaginal swab to check for bacteria

Prenatal Nutrition

  • Folic acid: 400-800 mcg daily (prevents neural tube defects)
  • Iron: 27 mg daily (prevents anemia)
  • Calcium: 1,000 mg daily (for baby's bone development)
  • DHA: 200-300 mg daily (supports brain development)
  • Protein: Additional 25g per day (71g total)
  • Calories: Add 300-500 extra calories per day (second and third trimesters)

Foods to Avoid During Pregnancy

  • Raw or undercooked seafood, eggs, and meat: Risk of foodborne illness
  • High-mercury fish: Shark, swordfish, king mackerel, tilefish
  • Unpasteurized dairy and juices: Risk of listeria
  • Deli meats and hot dogs: Unless heated until steaming
  • Raw sprouts: Risk of bacterial contamination
  • Alcohol: No safe amount during pregnancy
  • Excess caffeine: Limit to 200mg per day (about one 12oz coffee)
  • Unwashed produce: Risk of toxoplasmosis

Exercise During Pregnancy

Regular exercise during pregnancy offers many benefits:

  • Reduces back pain and improves posture
  • Decreases risk of gestational diabetes
  • Improves sleep and mood
  • Builds stamina for labor and delivery
  • Helps with healthy weight gain
  • Speeds postpartum recovery

Safe Pregnancy Exercises

  • Walking (30 minutes most days)
  • Swimming and water aerobics
  • Prenatal yoga and Pilates
  • Stationary cycling
  • Low-impact aerobics
  • Strength training with light weights

Exercises to Avoid

  • Contact sports (soccer, basketball, hockey)
  • Activities with fall risk (skiing, horseback riding, gymnastics)
  • Scuba diving
  • Hot yoga or hot Pilates
  • Exercise at high altitudes (above 6,000 feet)
  • Lying flat on your back after 20 weeks

Weight Gain Recommendations

Recommended weight gain during pregnancy depends on your pre-pregnancy BMI:

  • Underweight (BMI < 18.5): 28-40 pounds
  • Normal weight (BMI 18.5-24.9): 25-35 pounds
  • Overweight (BMI 25-29.9): 15-25 pounds
  • Obese (BMI ≥ 30): 11-20 pounds

Warning Signs - When to Call Your Doctor

Contact your healthcare provider immediately if you experience:

  • Vaginal bleeding or spotting (after first trimester)
  • Severe abdominal pain or cramping
  • Sudden swelling of face, hands, or feet
  • Severe or persistent headache
  • Vision changes (blurriness, spots, flashing lights)
  • High fever (over 101°F/38.3°C)
  • Painful or burning urination
  • Decreased fetal movement (after 28 weeks)
  • Fluid leaking from vagina (possible ruptured membranes)
  • Regular contractions before 37 weeks

Preparing for Baby's Arrival

Third Trimester Checklist

  • Take childbirth education class: Learn about labor, delivery, and newborn care
  • Create birth plan: Document your preferences for labor and delivery
  • Tour hospital or birth center: Familiarize yourself with the facility
  • Choose pediatrician: Schedule interviews and make selection
  • Install car seat: Have it inspected for proper installation
  • Pack hospital bag: Include essentials for you and baby (around 36 weeks)
  • Prepare nursery: Set up crib, changing table, and baby essentials
  • Stock up on supplies: Diapers, wipes, baby clothes, nursing supplies
  • Plan postpartum support: Arrange help for first weeks at home
  • Discuss maternity/paternity leave: Finalize plans with employer

Frequently Asked Questions

How accurate is my due date?

Due dates are estimates, and only about 5% of babies are born on their exact due date. Most babies are born between 37-42 weeks of pregnancy. Your due date may be adjusted after your dating ultrasound in the first trimester.

When will I start showing?

First-time moms typically start showing between 12-16 weeks. If this is your second or subsequent pregnancy, you may show earlier. Body type, baby's position, and muscle tone also affect when you show.

When can I find out the baby's sex?

An ultrasound can typically determine sex between 18-22 weeks. Some blood tests (cell-free DNA) can detect sex as early as 10 weeks, though these are optional screening tests primarily for genetic conditions.

Is it normal to not feel pregnant sometimes?

Yes, especially in the first trimester and between weeks 13-18 when symptoms often ease but movement isn't felt yet. If you have concerns or notice a sudden change in symptoms, contact your healthcare provider.

When will I feel the baby move?

First-time moms typically feel movement (quickening) between 18-22 weeks. Experienced moms may notice it earlier, around 16 weeks. Initially, it feels like flutters or bubbles.

Can I travel during pregnancy?

The safest time to travel is during the second trimester (weeks 14-27). Most airlines allow travel until 36 weeks for domestic flights. Always check with your healthcare provider before traveling, especially if you have complications.

How much water should I drink?

Aim for 8-12 cups (64-96 ounces) of water per day. Staying hydrated helps with common pregnancy issues like constipation, swelling, and overheating. Drink more if you're exercising or in hot weather.

What about morning sickness?

Despite the name, morning sickness can occur any time of day and affects about 70-80% of pregnant women. It typically peaks around weeks 9-10 and subsides by weeks 14-16. Tips for relief include eating small, frequent meals, staying hydrated, eating crackers before getting out of bed, and trying ginger or vitamin B6 supplements (ask your doctor first).
